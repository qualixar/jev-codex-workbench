# Publication comes after evidence

This package does not write or publish the article. It prepares material for that task.

## Claim rules

A fixture supports a claim about local application branching, not Jev intelligence.
A live synthetic receipt supports a claim about the recorded response to that input,
not enterprise adoption or general superiority. A type-valid answer can be wrong.
A proposed diagram is not an installed integration. A small coding demo is not a
production reliability benchmark. State these distinctions in the later article.

Separate reported vendor claims from your own measurements. Preserve model versions,
request/rubric/policy hashes, sample selection, errors and the test protocol. Do not
claim compliance certification, absence of vulnerabilities, complete injection defense,
end-to-end local inference, or proven customer outcomes.

## Early-access terms

Review the terms attached to your actual early-access account and any separate NDA
before publishing results. The public TypeSafe Master Customer Agreement reviewed on
2026-09-20, updated 2026-09-19, did **not** contain the blanket benchmark-publication
prohibition claimed in the earlier conversation. That earlier claim should not be
repeated. A separate account agreement may impose different obligations; this kit
cannot determine those private terms or grant publication permission.

## Privacy and rights checklist

Review all screenshots and every video frame, not just the first. Check account
identifiers, notifications, names, private paths, client labels, terminal history,
keys, requests and result payloads. Keep provenance labels intact. Confirm rights to
any captured third-party UI/assets and applicable early-access terms. No media is
marked public-ready automatically. Do not equate a regex pass with privacy clearance.

## Hand-back for the blog session

Bring `artifacts/exports/evidence-bundle.zip`, the final setup report, the results table,
your selected story angle, and any separately reviewed native Codex footage. Explain
which workflows ran live, which remained fixtures, and what unexpectedly failed.
Those are the inputs for credible architecture diagrams, an article and reel narration.
